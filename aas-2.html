The standard

One receipt. Three strengths of evidence.

An agreement chooses the level it needs. The format never changes — only what the receipt can prove without asking anyone to trust the provider.

Scope

What a receipt proves.

AAS-2 is deliberately narrow. It proves that a delivery matches an agreement, not that the content is true.

Proven

  • The delivered bytes match the format and rules the agreement named.
  • The receipt was signed by the key it carries, under the receipt domain.
  • At L3, the three memos exist in order on Solana and the clock is public.

Not proven

  • That the content is accurate or useful.
  • That a failed check is a legal breach.
  • That the verifier itself is honest — v0.1 trusts the signing key, and the registry makes it identifiable.

Draft 0.1. The version stays 0.x until an implementation written by someone else reproduces the pinned hashes, a verifier key rotation has been exercised, and the governance review is complete. The pinned fixtures are the contract.
